## Landing

The homepage presents Platonic Ideal as an independent product-research catalog: one product worth choosing per everyday category, or an honest explanation when no product qualifies. It is a public projection of the product and offer, so its claims must remain subordinate to the canonical dossier and its versioned evidence.

## Observations

- The public promise is explicit: “One product per category. Or none,” with independent judgment and public reasoning.
- The homepage says the free catalog covers 100 categories and that “the verdict and decisive reasoning are free.” It positions the dossier as paid research depth that never changes the ruling.
- The homepage advertises a `$24` digital research edition in preview language, but it does not identify the exact paid-edition coverage, a stable `dossier_id`, a dossier version, or a fulfillment path.
- The dossier promise is organized into four layers: the standard, the evidence, the tradeoffs, and the ownership plan. This gives the paid product a materially deeper shape than a category-card verdict, but the page does not yet show the coverage crosswalk.
- Featured public examples name four products: Lodge L10SK3 cast-iron skillet, Victorinox No. 8 stainless-steel blade, Parker Jotter Stainless Steel, and Wesco 2332HC boots. The homepage itself does not expose the corresponding dossier identifiers or revision dates.
- The public page includes an empty-state example for refrigerators and explains why a clear empty verdict is preferable to forcing a recommendation. This supports the one-product-or-none rule, but it remains a public editorial claim that needs a canonical case/ruling owner.
- The page discloses that its editorial illustrations are AI-generated interpretive plates, not product photography, and that physical products are not sold there. It also credits the Plato portrait and links the image license.
- The email section is explicitly sandbox-only: no address is saved, sent, or added to a list. The page names the production requirements of an approved provider, privacy notice, confirmation email, and unsubscribe route.
- The founder letter supplies the origin narrative in first person: first principles, Plato and Aristotle, reducing comparison load, and practicing AI building skills while keeping public judgment free. This is authority context, not evidence for any product verdict.
- Browser metadata identifies the page as `Platonic Ideal — Fewer things. Better reasons.` with a canonical URL, an independent durable/repairable-product description, Organization/WebSite/WebPage JSON-LD, and no console errors during retrieval.

## Contradictions / tensions

- **Public catalog versus paid-edition coverage:** 100 categories are advertised publicly, while the currently preserved declared PDF covers 48 recommendations. The site needs an explicit edition/coverage statement or generated association.
- **Public projection versus canonical authority:** the homepage states verdicts and decisive reasoning are free, but category pages and the offer do not yet visibly carry the dossier identity/version contract required by the goal objective.
- **Preview language versus production readiness:** the homepage names a `$24` digital edition and sandbox signup while the local storefront records the commercial flow as a design/local preview, not live payment, fulfillment, or real data collection.
