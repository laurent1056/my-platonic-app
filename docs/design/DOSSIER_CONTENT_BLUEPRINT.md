# Platonic Ideal Dossier content blueprint

Prepared 2026-09-15. This is the production brief for turning the current register into a paid digital publication with enough evidence, comparison, and ownership guidance to justify the offer. It complements `DOSSIER_PDF_ART_DIRECTION.md`; it does not claim that the unfinished dossier is already available.

## Product promise

The dossier should help a reader stop researching, buy with clear tradeoffs, and keep the object working. Its value comes from making the entire judgment inspectable:

- the Form and admission test for each category;
- the declared product, conditional answer, or honest empty finding;
- credible candidates and near-misses, including why each lost;
- model-specific construction, maintenance, parts, repair, and failure evidence;
- price and availability snapshots with dates and regions;
- direct links to useful manuals, parts, recalls, certifications, and deeper reading;
- original plates that make the decisive physical facts easier to understand;
- a source ledger, change log, and review date for every conclusion.

The reader is paying for compressed judgment and a durable reference, not a longer version of the free category pages.

## Edition architecture

Plan the first complete edition as roughly 180–220 pages. Final pagination follows the evidence, not an arbitrary page count.

| Section | Approximate pages | Reader value |
|---|---:|---|
| Cover, colophon, how to use the dossier | 6–8 | Establish scope, dates, symbols, and link behavior |
| The Constitution and decision method | 12–16 | Explain Forms, evidence rules, confidence, and why `EMPTY` is a useful verdict |
| Nine domain openers | 18 | Plato or Greek-antiquity image, short sourced passage, domain map, and reading path |
| Forty-eight declared category spreads | 96 | Two-page product plates with winner, comparisons, ownership, and sources |
| Twenty-four candidate briefs | 24–36 | Ranked field, missing evidence, and what could change the verdict |
| Twelve empty findings | 12–18 | Near-misses, structural failure pattern, and a specification for what would qualify |
| Conditional, consumable, and split findings | 16–24 | Use-case matrix, narrower Forms, and rational replacement guidance |
| Indices, source ledger, credits, and change log | 14–22 | Product, category, price, repair, source, image, and quotation retrieval |

The PDF should include bookmarks, a linked contents page, back-links from notes, searchable text, tagged headings, alt text for meaningful images, and visible page numbers. External links need readable labels in addition to clickable URLs.

## The declared-category spread

Each declared category receives a consistent two-page spread.

### Left page: the judgment

1. Reference code, category, state, confidence, last reviewed date
2. Form definition in one precise sentence
3. Exact declared model, variant, region, and price snapshot
4. Large identity plate with visible truth label
5. “Why this ends the search” in 80–140 words
6. Five decisive facts, each paired with its ownership consequence
7. Compact dimensions, materials, warranty, country of manufacture when verified

### Right page: the ownership case

1. Construction, operation, or ownership plate
2. Admission test with pass evidence for each criterion
3. Three to five named runners-up and one-line loss reasons
4. Failure modes: symptom, likely cause, consequence, repair path, terminal threshold
5. Maintenance calendar and expected consumables
6. Parts and service path, including common part numbers when stable
7. Five- and ten-year cost view where the category warrants it
8. Six to twelve annotated links, with source role and access date
9. “What would change our mind” statement

If evidence is thin, shorten the spread and say so. Page count must never disguise uncertainty.

## Candidate and near-miss system

The target is at least three credible named products per category, yielding 300 or more candidate-level records across the 100-category edition. Do not pad a category with irrelevant products to hit the number.

Each candidate record includes:

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Identity | Manufacturer, exact model, variant, region, stable product ID |
| Role | Winner, finalist, near-miss, use-case exception, legacy benchmark, or watchlist |
| Evidence state | Verified, partially verified, contradicted, unavailable, or stale |
| Price | Amount or range, currency, seller type, region, capture date |
| Admission test | Pass, fail, unknown, and cited reason for every criterion |
| Decisive advantage | The strongest reason a reader might choose it |
| Decisive loss | The reason it does not receive the declaration |
| Ownership | Maintenance, wear parts, service path, warranty, likely failure modes |
| Availability | Current, regional, discontinued, used-only, or announced |
| Sources | Annotated links with access dates and archived copies where appropriate |
| Reconsideration trigger | The new fact, revision, or market change that would reopen it |

The final comparison table should distinguish fact from editorial judgment. Measurements and policy terms are facts with sources; “best balance” and “too dependent on proprietary service” are judgments supported by those facts.

## Source bundle for every category

Each category should have a compact, annotated reference bundle. A link earns space because it helps the owner decide, verify, repair, or revisit the judgment.

### Primary sources

- Exact manufacturer product and specification page
- Owner, installation, service, and parts manuals
- Exploded parts diagrams and official replacement-parts listings
- Warranty and software-support policy
- Safety notices and the [U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission recall search](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/)
- Certification and performance registries where relevant: [ENERGY STAR Product Finder](https://www.energystar.gov/productfinder/index), the European Commission's [EPREL public database](https://eprel.ec.europa.eu/), the [EPEAT Registry](https://www.epeat.net/), and [TCO Certified Product Finder](https://tcocertified.com/search/)
- Patents, regulatory filings, and exact-model technical documents when they explain construction or interoperability

### Independent and ownership sources

- Instrumented tests with disclosed methods and the exact tested model
- Long-term reviews with meaningful ownership duration
- Teardowns, repair guides, and scoring methods such as [iFixit's repairability index](https://www.ifixit.com/repairability)
- Specialist forums and owner communities for recurring symptoms, parts substitutions, and service experience
- Used-market listings for discontinued availability and real replacement economics

Forum posts, retailer copy, reviews, and AI summaries may point toward evidence. They do not establish a fact by themselves. Record disagreements instead of averaging them into certainty.

### Link annotation pattern

Use a short, readable label and explain why the source matters:

> **Service manual · manufacturer · accessed 2026-09-15** — Exploded diagram, maintenance intervals, fastener sizes, and replacement procedure for the exact variant. [Open source]

For pages likely to change, record the title, publisher, publication or revision date, access date, region, and an archival URL when permitted. Price links always show the capture date and never masquerade as stable specifications.

## Evidence ledger

The manuscript needs a structured ledger rather than footnotes assembled by hand. Each claim should be traceable to a source ID.

Minimum source fields:

```text
source_id
category_id
candidate_id
source_role
title
publisher_or_creator
url
archive_url
published_or_revised
accessed
region
exact_model_match
independence_class
rights_status
claims_supported
quotation_or_page_locator
notes
```

Minimum claim fields:

```text
claim_id
category_id
candidate_id
claim_text
claim_type
source_ids
verification_state
reviewer
reviewed_at
publication_note
```

One source may support several claims, but every important claim needs a locator or a precise explanation of what was verified. Broken-link checks belong in the publication build.

## Ownership value modules

Use these modules where they materially improve the decision:

- **Before first use:** setup, seasoning, calibration, break-in, registration, safety inspection
- **Maintenance clock:** after each use, monthly, yearly, and condition-based tasks
- **Failure atlas:** symptom-to-cause table with safe diagnostic boundaries
- **Parts map:** wear parts, common fasteners, generic equivalents, proprietary dependencies
- **Repair economics:** typical part cost, skill, tools, time, service cost, and replacement threshold
- **Compatibility:** dimensions, interfaces, consumables, accessories, chargers, batteries, blades, filters, or software versions
- **Used-buying inspection:** serial/model check, recall check, wear indicators, missing pieces, and walk-away conditions
- **Storage and transport:** factors that prevent corrosion, deformation, discharge, or material decay
- **End of service:** resale, rebuild, recycling, hazardous-material, or responsible disposal path

The dossier must state when a task needs a qualified technician or when a safety-critical product should not be opened by the owner.

## Cost and time horizon

Where purchase price is a poor proxy for value, include a transparent ownership model:

```text
ten_year_cost = purchase_price
              + expected_consumables
              + scheduled_maintenance
              + probability_weighted_repairs
              - residual_value
```

Show assumptions next to the result. Use ranges when service life or repair frequency is uncertain. Do not invent a precise lifetime from anecdotes.

## Delta-inspired illustration package

For each declared model, commission the plate set defined in `DOSSIER_PDF_ART_DIRECTION.md`. The visual reference packet may include exact-model manufacturer photographs, manuals, parts diagrams, patents, measurements, and owned photographs. It remains an internal research packet unless the rights ledger permits republication.

The ideal sequence is:

1. lock the exact model and variant;
2. complete the admission test and source bundle;
3. choose the one mechanism or ownership fact worth visualizing;
4. prepare a visual fact sheet from multiple sources;
5. create original exterior and, when justified, construction artwork;
6. compare the plate to exact-model references;
7. run a mechanical truth review and a rights review;
8. publish with an illustration truth label and source IDs.

Plato and open images of Greek antiquity should mark chapter transitions and the philosophical frame. They should not compete with product evidence inside category spreads.

## Perceived value that survives scrutiny

The edition should make its depth easy to see and easy to use:

- linked domain, category, product, and model indices;
- “best for” and “avoid if” paths without diluting the single declaration;
- a repairability and parts-availability index;
- a price-band and expected-cost index;
- a maintenance calendar appendix;
- a tool and consumables appendix;
- a consolidated recall and safety-check directory;
- a dated “what changed since the last edition” page;
- a machine-readable source ledger supplied with the PDF when practical;
- a short update policy that distinguishes factual corrections from changed verdicts.

No bonus section should exist only to increase page count. Every module must reduce search time, expose evidence, or improve ownership.

## Production sequence

### Phase 1 — harden the data

- Add candidate, source, claim, price-snapshot, and illustration-ledger schemas.
- Fill admission tests and failure modes for all 100 categories.
- Replace placeholder image URLs with internal art status and source-ledger references.
- Resolve current notes that flag category/model mismatches or stale claims.

### Phase 2 — declared categories

- Research at least three alternatives for each of the 48 declarations.
- Verify exact variants, current availability, maintenance, parts, warranty, recalls, and ownership costs.
- Draft and source the 48 two-page spreads before commissioning the full illustration set.

### Phase 3 — unresolved categories

- Rank the 24 candidate fields and name the missing evidence.
- Turn the 10 split categories into narrower Forms with independent admission tests.
- Give the 12 empty findings named near-misses and a concrete specification for what would qualify.
- Make the four conditional and two consumable decisions explicit by use case and replacement logic.

### Phase 4 — art, edit, and build

- Produce the product plates and nine antiquity-led domain openers.
- Run mechanical truth, citation, rights, accessibility, copy, and layout reviews.
- Build the tagged PDF, validate links and bookmarks, and inspect every page at normal reading size.
- Publish only after checkout, fulfillment, support, update policy, and final terms match the actual file.

## Release gates

The dossier is ready to sell when:

- all 100 categories have a complete admission test, failure modes, ownership path, and review date;
- every category has a defensible comparison field or a documented reason that credible alternatives do not exist;
- every declared model has current identity, variant, availability, manual, service/parts, warranty, recall, and price evidence where applicable;
- every material factual claim resolves to a source ID and every external link passes;
- every image has provenance, rights status, alt text, and an illustration truth label;
- no internal component, dimension, certification, quote, price, or service-life claim is inferred from appearance alone;
- the PDF passes visual, structural accessibility, link, metadata, file-size, and print tests;
- the storefront accurately states edition date, coverage, file format, page count, delivery, update terms, refund terms, and support channel.

Until those gates pass, the storefront remains a design preview and the $24 figure remains a design reference.
