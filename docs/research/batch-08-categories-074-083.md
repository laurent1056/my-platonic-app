# Constitutional Batch 08 — Categories 74–83

**Research date:** 2026-09-08  
**Constitution:** 1.1.0  
**Backlog span:** Outdoor 74–80, Electronics 81–83

## What this batch is

This is a constitutional re-adjudication and expansion pass, not a blind
append to the inherited CSV. The inherited register is useful historical
material, but it is not proof that a row has passed the institution. Each
recommendation below defines the Form first, then asks whether one exact
product can end the search inside that Form.

The Constitution's declaration-seeking standard is applied as follows:

- `EMPTY` is a last resort, not the default answer for difficult categories.
- Ordinary trade-offs, replaceable wear parts, maintenance, and rational
  renewal are not automatic failures.
- A broad category may need a child Form before it can receive a meaningful
  declaration.
- The batch's recommendations are **not terminal public rulings yet**. They
  are research dispositions that should become `CANDIDATE` case files, then
  terminal `DECLARED` rulings only after the evidence packet, counter-case,
  confidence floor, adjudicator, and public receipt are complete.

## The ten determinations

### 74 — Sleeping Bag

**Form:** A three-season backpacking sleeping bag for a regular-length adult,
with a nominal rating around 20°F, premium down insulation, a mummy shape, and
an owner-usable path for cleaning, repair, and long-term loft maintenance.
Exclude winter expedition bags, summer quilts, synthetic bags, and bags whose
fit or temperature range changes the job.

**Proposed declaration:** Western Mountaineering **UltraLite**, 20°F, regular
length. The exact terminal record must capture the selected length and zipper
side rather than treating the entire product page's variants as one SKU.

**Why it survives the rescue pass:** the Form is coherent; the 20°F rating is
an operating constraint rather than a universal claim; and ordinary wear does
not require discarding the bag. The current product page identifies a 20°F
mummy bag, 850+ fill-power goose down, 12D ripstop shell, YKK #5 zipper, and
regular-length measurements. Western Mountaineering documents factory repairs,
an unlimited-time manufacturing-defect guarantee, and a San Jose repair path.
Down loss, shell punctures, zipper trouble, and drawcord or seam damage are
maintenance events, not automatic category failures.

**Counter-case / open proof:** Feathered Friends, Katabatic Gear, and a good
synthetic bag may win for different Forms. The case must show why those are
not competing answers to this exact 20°F premium-down backpacking Form. Before
terminal issue, capture the current exact variant identifier, independent
field evidence, and the economic boundary for a major down rebuild.

**Sources:** [UltraLite product page](https://www.westernmountaineering.com/product/ultralite/),
[specification chart](https://www.westernmountaineering.com/about/sleeping-bags-specification-chart/),
[warranty](https://www.westernmountaineering.com/support/guarantee-warranty/),
[repairs](https://www.westernmountaineering.com/repairs/),
[company information](https://www.westernmountaineering.com/about/).

### 75 — Cooler

**Form:** A portable, hard-sided cooler for car camping, day boating, hunting,
or a similar ordinary field use, in the roughly 40–50 quart class. Exclude
soft coolers, powered refrigerators, and expedition-scale chests where weight
and capacity change the governing Form.

**Proposed declaration:** YETI **Tundra 45 Hard Cooler**, product
`10045020000`.

**Why it survives the rescue pass:** the Tundra 45 has a stable, exact model
identity; rotomolded single-piece construction; thick insulation; a gasket;
hinges; latches; drain hardware; and a published five-year warranty. YETI
explicitly identifies the Tundra 45 as a general-purpose size, with 54-can or
37-pound-ice capacity. The maker also documents that T-latches and gaskets
have basic repair paths and sells replacement latch parts. The permanence
mechanism is durable body construction plus serviceable interfaces and
warranty support, not a claim that a crushed polyethylene shell can be
rebuilt indefinitely.

**Counter-case / open proof:** RTIC offers similar construction at a lower
price; Pelican may offer a different latch and warranty proposition. The case
must compare insulation, hinge/latch service, drain replacement, warranty
scope, weight, and repair economics rather than treating brand reputation as
the verdict. Heavy weight and price are trade-offs, not disqualifiers.

**Sources:** [YETI Tundra 45](https://www.yeti.com/coolers/hard-coolers/tundra/10045020000.html),
[Tundra owner's manual](https://www.yeti.com/tundra-owners-manual.html),
[YETI warranty information](https://www.yeti.com/warranty-information.html),
[replacement T-Rex latches](https://www.yeti.com/coolers/coolers-accessories/20110010013.html).

### 76 — Water Bottle

**Form:** A single-wall, reusable stainless-steel bottle for ordinary daily
carry, roughly 24–32 ounces, with a body that does not depend on a coating and
a cap system that can be replaced. Exclude insulated bottles, cycling-specific
bottles, filtration bottles, and glass bottles; each has a different failure
and permanence logic.

**Proposed declaration:** Klean Kanteen **Classic 27 oz with the current Sport
Cap configuration**. The final case must capture the current product/SKU and
cap variant, because “Classic 27 oz” is a product family with interchangeable
closures rather than one complete object.

**Why it survives the rescue pass:** the stainless body is the durable core;
the cap, gasket, and straw are rational wear components; and Klean Kanteen
publishes compatible cap families and replacement maintenance parts. A cap
wearing out is not a reason to discard the vessel. The Form is intentionally
not “the best bottle for every hydration situation”; it is the everyday,
single-wall bottle that ends the search for that job.

**Counter-case / open proof:** the all-stainless or plastic-free cap variant
may have a stronger permanence story than the sport cap. We should compare
the current cap options and select one exact configuration, then verify the
limited-warranty terms, gasket availability, and independent leak/durability
evidence before issuing a terminal declaration.

**Sources:** [Classic 27 oz product page](https://www.kleankanteen.com/products/water-bottle-classic-27-oz),
[plastic-free 27 oz variant](https://www.kleankanteen.com/collections/classic/products/plastic-free-water-bottle-27oz),
[FAQ and cap compatibility](https://www.kleankanteen.com/pages/faq),
[maintenance kits and replacement parts](https://www.kleankanteen.com/collections/maintenance-kits).

### 77 — Camping Stove

**Form:** A liquid-fuel backpacking stove for multi-fuel field use, where
field maintenance, cold-weather capability, and a serviceable pump/burner
system matter more than push-button convenience. Exclude canister-only stoves,
household camp kitchens, and indoor appliances.

**Proposed declaration:** MSR **WhisperLite International**, SKU `06633`.

**Why it survives the rescue pass:** the stove is a serviceable system rather
than a sealed appliance. MSR's manual identifies replaceable O-rings, pump
seals, jets, the generator, fuel-line parts, and a jet-and-cable tool. The
Annual Maintenance Kit and Expedition Service Kit provide a documented path
for normal renewal and service. Priming, cleaning, and fuel handling are
ordinary operating requirements for this Form, not hidden disqualifiers.

**Non-negotiable safety boundary:** the stove is for outdoor use only. The
manual warns about carbon monoxide, fire, burns, leaks, and using only
model-specific replacement parts. Those warnings must be displayed in the
public ruling. A product that is maintainable but unsafe when misused is not
therefore EMPTY; its use boundary must be explicit.

**Counter-case / open proof:** MSR WhisperLite Universal and DragonFly may win
for different fuel or simmering requirements. The case should verify current
availability, current warranty/service terms, fuel compatibility, and one
independent field review before terminal issue.

**Sources:** [WhisperLite International manual](https://www.msrgear.com/on/demandware.static/-/Library-Sites-cdiSharedLibrary/default/dw0ca15003/pdf/manuals/06633_WLI_Instr_33-945_ENG.pdf),
[WhisperLite Expedition Service Kit manual](https://www.msrgear.com/on/demandware.static/-/Library-Sites-cdiSharedLibrary/default/dwb7fe1775/pdf/manuals/msr_whisperlite_expedition_service_kit_manual.pdf),
[MSR maintenance video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e_2qN3ZTQSE).

### 78 — Backpacking Pack

**Form:** A multi-day backpacking pack in the 60–70 liter class, designed for
approximately 30–40 pounds of load, with an adjustable/fitted torso and a
repair path for fabric, harness, frame, and hardware. Exclude daypacks,
ultralight frameless packs, expedition haulers, and packs whose load range is
materially different.

**Proposed declaration:** Osprey **Atmos AG 65**, with the user's torso-size
variant recorded as part of the Form. The current product lineage is
`Atmos AG (2022-current)`; the case must record the exact size and current
model identifier rather than pretending S/M and L/XL are interchangeable.

**Why it survives the rescue pass:** fit is an essential Form constraint, not
a reason to abandon the category. The official specifications put the pack
at 65/68 liters with a 30–40 pound load range. Osprey's All Mighty Guarantee
states that it will repair or replace a pack at no cost for any reason and
accepts pack name, volume, frame/size, and damage details as part of the repair
process. Fabric abrasion, buckles, zippers, foam, and suspension components
are service events to investigate; they are not presumed terminal.

**Counter-case / open proof:** ULA Circuit and Osprey Aether 65 represent
lighter and heavier-load Forms. They are serious counter-cases only if the
Form is widened beyond 30–40 pounds. The final case needs current US product
availability, independent fit/load evidence, and a concrete replacement-parts
record.

**Sources:** [Atmos AG 65 product page](https://www.osprey.com/gb/osprey-atmos-ag-65-s22),
[official Atmos/Aura manual](https://www.osprey.com/gb/media/user_manuals/OM_Atmos_Aura_S22_ENG.pdf),
[Osprey All Mighty Guarantee](https://www.osprey.com/customer-support/all-mighty-guarantee),
[current 65 L category](https://www.osprey.com/eu/backpacks-bags/65l-backpacks).

### 79 — Pocket Knife

**Disposition:** Preserve `SPLIT_REQUIRED`. This is not an EMPTY result and
not a reason to resurrect the inherited CSV declaration as if it were already
constitutional. The founding case is [PI-C-0001](../../cases/PI-C-0001.json),
pinned to Constitution 1.1.0.

The existing evidence shows two legitimate Forms:

1. **Traditional Utility Folding Knife** — a heavier, traditional folder such
   as the Buck 110, where sheath/kit carry and factory service matter.
2. **Modern Everyday Pocket Knife** — a lighter pocket-compatible folder such
   as an Opinel No. 8 or Spyderco Delica, where pocket access, weight, edge
   maintenance, and rational renewal matter.

The declaration-seeking move is to adjudicate those successor Forms, not to
force Buck 110, Opinel, and Delica into a false universal category. The next
work is two successor case files and a decision about whether blade replacement
is essential or whether recoverable sharpening/re-profiling is enough for each
Form.

### 80 — Hunting Knife

**Form:** A general-purpose fixed-blade hunting/field knife for ordinary belt
or sheath carry, with a durable stainless blade, maintainable edge, and a
stable sheath/service path. Exclude dedicated skinning knives, kitchen knives,
tactical/combat knives, and bushcraft tools whose primary geometry changes the
job.

**Proposed declaration:** Buck **119 Special**, provisional model identifier
`0119BKS-B`.

**Why it survives the rescue pass:** fixed-blade simplicity is itself a
permanence mechanism. The blade can be sharpened and, if necessary,
re-profiled; the handle and guard have fewer interfaces than a folding knife;
and the sheath is a replaceable carry component. Buck documents a life-of-knife
defect warranty, factory service, sharpening/polish/adjust service, and a long
classic lineage for the 119. The fact that Buck does not offer blade
replacement for fixed blades is not automatically a hard disqualifier: blade
replacement is not essential to this Form if ordinary edge recovery and
rational factory repair are sufficient.

**Counter-case / open proof:** the Buck 124, 105 Pathfinder, and dedicated
skinners may outperform the 119 for different tasks. Before terminal issue,
verify the exact current 119 model identifier and specifications from Buck,
add an independent field/use source, and state the legal/safety boundary for
knife carry and hunting use.

**Sources:** [Buck's Forever Warranty](https://www.buckknives.com/about/behind-the-blade/bucks-forever-warranty/),
[Buck gift guide/current 119 listing](https://www.buckknives.com/collections/gift-guide/),
[Buck care guidance](https://www.buckknives.com/about/behind-the-blade/knife-care/),
[Buck usage and safety](https://www.buckknives.com/about/behind-the-blade/usage-safety/).

### 81 — Smartphone

**Parent disposition:** The inherited broad `Smartphone = EMPTY` claim is too
wide under Constitution 1.1.0. The declaration-rescue pass finds a coherent
child Form rather than a reason to keep the whole category empty.

**Child Form:** A current Android smartphone sold in an official Fairphone
market, with long software support, user-replaceable battery/display/ports,
published repair guides, and a service path for the core module. Exclude
flagship-camera competition, gaming phones, and unsupported gray-market
imports. The market and support jurisdiction must be part of the public
statement.

**Proposed child declaration:** Fairphone **(Gen. 6+)**. Fairphone documents
12 user-replaceable spare parts, a removable back cover, a five-year warranty,
software support through 2033, and an A repairability rating. The core module
is service-center work, which must be disclosed rather than hidden; a phone
does not need every component to be user-replaceable to have a meaningful
repairable Form.

**Counter-case / open proof:** the strongest counter-case is that Fairphone's
market footprint and core-module dependence make a universal `Smartphone`
declaration misleading. The right response is a child Form and an explicit
jurisdiction, not EMPTY. Verify US availability/support separately before
claiming this result applies to the US audience.

**Sources:** [Fairphone Gen. 6 / 6+](https://www.fairphone.com/the-fairphone-gen-6),
[spare-part repair guide](https://support.fairphone.com/hc/en-us/articles/24590029621138-The-Fairphone-Gen-6-Replace-a-Spare-Part),
[Fairphone repairs](https://www.fairphone.com/repairs),
[Fairphone warranty](https://www.fairphone.com/warranty).

### 82 — Laptop

**Form:** A general-purpose laptop for work, writing, development, and
ordinary Linux or Windows use, where the battery, display, keyboard, ports,
storage, memory, and mainboard have an explicit replacement or upgrade path.
Exclude gaming performance as the governing criterion and exclude machines
whose portability or specialized graphics needs create a different Form.

**Proposed declaration:** Framework **Laptop 13 DIY Edition, AMD Ryzen AI 300
Series**, with one exact processor configuration recorded in the case (the
Ryzen AI 7 350 is the current working choice).

**Why it survives the rescue pass:** Framework's current comparison page
identifies the 2025 AMD AI 300 platform and individual configurations; the
company publishes parts, repair guides, and a warranty process that can supply
replacement modules. Framework's right-to-repair architecture—visible
fasteners, labeled modules, and one-tool service—directly addresses the
category's usual battery, keyboard, display, port, and storage failure modes.
The one-year warranty is not being mistaken for a lifetime guarantee; the
permanence case is the parts-and-guides ecosystem.

**Counter-case / open proof:** a used ThinkPad may have stronger historical
serviceability, and a MacBook may win on performance or battery efficiency.
Those are counter-cases to different Forms. The terminal packet still needs
an independent long-term assessment and a current parts-availability check.

**Sources:** [Framework Laptop 13](https://frame.work/laptop13?tab=resources-and-guides),
[current AMD configurations](https://frame.work/products/laptop13-diy-amd-ai300/comparison),
[Framework warranty](https://frame.work/warranty),
[right-to-repair design](https://frame.work/blog/preparing-to-ship-and-respecting-your-right-to-repair).

### 83 — Desktop Computer

**Parent disposition:** The broad category contains incompatible Forms:
small quiet desktop, expandable workstation, gaming tower, Linux-first
developer machine, and server. It should not receive one universal verdict.
The rescue pass therefore creates a child Form rather than preserving the old
`CONDITIONAL` row indefinitely or issuing a dishonest generic declaration.

**Child Form:** A small-footprint, repairable desktop for general work,
Linux, and local compute, where standard storage, power, cooling, and
motherboard interfaces matter more than maximum discrete-GPU expansion.

**Proposed child declaration:** Framework **Desktop**, Ryzen AI Max+ 395 with
64 GB memory, current platform configuration.

**Why it survives the rescue pass:** Framework identifies a 4.5-liter design
using a standard Mini-ITX mainboard, FlexATX power supply, 120 mm fan, M.2
storage, and configurable expansion cards. The platform can run Linux or
Windows and is designed to be assembled and serviced. Soldered memory is a
real limitation and must remain in the public ruling; it is a Form trade-off,
not automatically a hard disqualifier when the selected 64 GB configuration
is adequate for the defined job.

**Counter-case / open proof:** System76's Thelio Mira is a serious counter-case
for a larger Linux desktop because its published repair documentation covers
GPU, RAM, M.2 storage, CPU/cooling, PSU, and I/O replacement. The case must
explain why the small-footprint Form selects Framework, or split Linux desktop
from small modular desktop before terminal issue.

**Sources:** [Framework Desktop overview](https://frame.work/desktop/?tab=overview),
[Framework Desktop specs](https://frame.work/desktop?tab=specs),
[System76 desktops](https://system76.com/desktops),
[Thelio Mira repair documentation](https://www.system76.com/tech-docs/models/thelio-mira-r4-n4/repairs).
