# **Product Requirements Document (PRD)**

**Product Name:** Platonic Ideal  
**Version:** v1.0 (Index Phase)  
**Status:** Ready for Build

## **1. Product Summary (One Sentence)**

Platonic Ideal is a curated index that declares the single product in each category that is built to last, can be repaired when it fails, and ends the need to keep shopping—or leaves the category empty when nothing qualifies.

## **2. Problem Statement**

Modern consumers face:

  - Endless choice
  - Rapid product degradation
  - Hidden obsolescence
  - Marketing-driven “upgrades”
  - Review sites that optimize for clicks, not finality

There is **no trusted place** that:

  - Names *one* object per category
  - Refuses personalization, ranking, or alternatives
  - Treats emptiness as an honest outcome
  - Applies durability and repairability as non-negotiable standards

## **3. Product Goals**

### **Primary Goals**

1.  **End the search** for durable, honest objects
2.  **Create trust** through refusal, scarcity, and transparency
3.  **Establish a standard**, not a marketplace

### **Non-Goals (Explicit)**

  - Not a review site
  - Not a shopping guide
  - Not optimized for conversion
  - Not personalized (“best for you” is out of scope)
  - Not comprehensive across all products

## **4. Target Users**

### **Primary Users (v1)**

  - Founder (you)
  - Family + 3 friends (adversarial council)
  - Early readers who value durability, repairability, and restraint

### **Secondary Users (future)**

  - “Buy it for life” consumers
  - Craftspeople, mechanics, repair-minded users
  - Readers fatigued by recommendation algorithms

## **5. Core Product Principles (Non-Negotiable)**

1.  **One Ideal per Category****  
    **No lists. No runners-up. No substitutes.
2.  **Finality Over Optimization****  
    **The product should end the search, not start comparison.
3.  **Reversible Failure Modes****  
    **If it breaks, it can be fixed in a rational way.
4.  **Model Stability Matters****  
    **Silent drift disqualifies products.
5.  **Emptiness Is Integrity****  
    **Categories may remain empty indefinitely.
6.  **Revocation Is Public****  
    **Declarations can be withdrawn when reality changes.

## **6. Product Scope (v1)**

### **In Scope**

  - Public-facing index website
  - \~15 declared categories/products
  - Empty categories allowed
  - Written declarations with evidence
  - Clear revocation policy

### **Out of Scope (v1)**

  - User accounts
  - Comments
  - Ratings
  - Search filters
  - Affiliate optimization
  - E-commerce
  - Personalization

## **7. Content Model (Canonical)**

Each **Category Page** must include:

1.  **Form Statement****  
    **One sentence defining the essential function of the category.
2.  **Declaration Status**
      
      - Declared
      - Candidate
      - Empty
      - Deprecated
3.  **The Ideal (if declared)**
      
      - Exact product name
      - Manufacturer
      - Model identifier
4.  **Why This**
      
      - Structural reasons (materials, construction, ecosystem)
5.  **Why Not Others**
      
      - Disqualifiers (coatings, sealed systems, drift, etc.)
6.  **Failure Modes**
      
      - How it fails
      - Whether failures are reversible
      - When replacement becomes rational
7.  **Evidence**
      
      - Repair paths
      - Parts availability
      - Known longevity data
      - Service ecosystem
8.  **Flags (Optional, Standardized)**
      
      - Supply-Constrained
      - Niche Use
      - Fuel-Inefficient
      - Service Latency
      - Aging Platform
9.  **Where to Buy (Optional, Non-Prominent)**
      
      - Informational only in v1

## **8. Category States & Rules**

### **Declared**

  - Meets all admission tests
  - Ends the search

### **Candidate**

  - Promising but missing evidence
  - Not yet endorsed

### **Empty**

  - Market structurally compromised
  - No honest declaration possible

### **Deprecated**

  - Formerly declared
  - Disqualified due to drift, parts loss, or new failure modes

## **9. Revocation Policy (Public-Facing)**

A declaration is revoked if:

  - Materials or construction change materially
  - Parts/service become unavailable
  - New irreversible failure modes appear
  - Economic repair no longer makes sense
  - Ethical or regulatory issues emerge

Revoked pages remain visible with:

  - Revocation reason
  - Date
  - Evidence

## **10. MVP Success Criteria**

The MVP is considered successful if:

  - At least **15 categories** exist
  - At least **8–10 are declared**
  - At least **3–5 are empty**
  - Early users can correctly explain:
      
      - Why emptiness matters
      - Why there is only one product
      - Why no alternatives are given

A key success signal:

Users start using the site’s criteria to judge products elsewhere.

## **11. Technical Requirements (High-Level)**

### **v1 Architecture**

  - Content-first (Markdown or structured files)
  - Static or near-static site
  - Version-controlled content
  - Minimal CSS, fixed typography
  - No database required initially

### **Performance**

  - Fast load
  - Readable on mobile
  - Printable pages

## **12. Future Considerations (Not v1)**

  - Affiliate links (only for declared products)
  - PDF/book export
  - “Historically Declarable” archive
  - Manufacturer partnerships (non-exclusive)
  - Public submission of challenges (controlled)

## **13. Open Questions (Tracked, Not Blocking)**

  - Exact number of initial categories
  - How to label aging-but-still-declarable products
  - Whether to show “last reviewed” dates
  - Whether to add a “challenge the declaration” workflow

## **14. Current Status Summary**

  - ✅ Concept validated
  - ✅ Rulebook written
  - ✅ Hard categories stress-tested
  - ✅ Emptiness proven
  - ✅ Declarations viable
  - ✅ Ready to build v1

  - One-sentence definition (already written)

**Function:** Browsing, not shopping

      - Declared
      - Empty
      - Candidate

  - “Declared: 9 categories”

**Role:** This is your “product detail page”

  - One sentence explaining why the category fails

This answers the user’s question immediately:

“Is there something here or not?”

#### **B. Form Statement**

**Function:** Replace “Product Description”

  - One sentence
  - Defines the essential function
  - Implies disqualifiers

This trains the reader how to think.

#### **C. “Why This” (If Declared)**

**Function:** Replace feature bullets

Borrow from:

  - Longevity
  - Repairability
  - Ecosystem

#### **D. “Why Not Others”**

**Function:** Replace comparison tables

Borrow from:

  - “Why we’re different” sections

Used to:

  - Name common alternatives
  - Explain structural failure, not preference

This is where trust compounds.

#### **E. Failure Modes**

**Function:** Replace warranty section

Borrow from:

  - Warranty / returns info area

Explicitly states:

  - How the product fails
  - What is reversible
  - When replacement becomes rational

#### **F. Evidence**

**Function:** Replace reviews

Borrow from:

  - Spec tabs / documentation

Includes:

  - Parts availability
  - Service paths
  - Known longevity
  - Stability notes

#### **G. Flags (If Any)**

**Function:** Replace shipping notices / disclaimers

  - Niche Use

  - Aging Platform

#### **H. “Where to Buy” (Optional, Subtle)**

**Function:** Replace “Add to Cart”

Rules:

  - Not visually dominant
  - No urgency language
  - No price emphasis
  - No multiple retailers competing

This keeps commerce subordinate to judgment.

## **3. Empty Category Page**

**Role:** Prove integrity  
**Mental Model Borrowed From:** “Out of stock” / discontinued product page

This page is **critical** to credibility.

### **What it must do**

### **Structure**

  - Form Statement (still exists)
  - Disqualifiers (why market fails)

### **Function**

  - Explain *why* the site refuses abundance
  - Explain why emptiness is honest
  - Explain revocation

This is **not** marketing copy.  
It’s governance.

Should answer:

  - “Why should I trust this?”
  - “What happens when they’re wrong?”

## **5. How We Decide (Methodology Page)**

**Role:** Replace “review policy” pages

### **Contents**

## **6. Deprecated / Revoked Page (Archive)**

3.  *Can I trust it?*

Every element on the homepage must answer one of these.

  - Marketplace homepages (Amazon, IKEA)
  - Editorial indexes (NYT sections)
  - High-trust brand landers (Patagonia)

## **6. Evidence**

  - Parts availability
  - Service ecosystem
  - Repair documentation
  - Longevity examples
  - Model stability notes

### **Rules**

  - No anecdotes without context
  - No star ratings
  - No testimonials

Evidence should feel boring.  
That’s a feature.

  - Niche Use
  - Fuel-Inefficient
  - Service Latency
  - Aging Platform

### **Rules**

  - Flags are finite and predefined
  - No custom one-offs
  - Flags explain friction, not failure

## **8. Where to Buy (Optional, De-Emphasized)**

**Function:****  
**Replace Add-to-Cart.

### **Rules**

  - Secondary visual weight
  - No urgency language
  - No price anchoring
  - One or two sources max

The declaration must stand **without** this section.

4.  An empty page feels honest, not lacking

  - One template, four states
  - Content-driven, not interactive
  - Print-friendly
  - Stable URLs
  - No comments or dynamic widgets

## **Summary**

The Category Page is a **judgment engine** wrapped in a familiar interface.

It looks like a product page so users know where to look—  
but it behaves like a standard, not a salesperson.

  
  
  

# Empty Category  

# **\#3 Empty Category Page — Deep Definition**

## **Role of the Empty Category Page (Non-Negotiable)**

The Empty Category Page has **one job**:

**Prove that refusal is intentional, reasoned, and stable—not a lack of effort.**

This page must make the user think:

“If they’re willing to say *nothing qualifies*, I trust them when they say *something does*.”

Empty pages are **not placeholders**.  
They are **published judgments**.

## **Mental Model Borrowed**

**Borrowed from:**

  - “Out of Stock” pages (done right)
  - Product recall notices
  - Editorial corrections
  - Regulatory findings

**But inverted:**

  - Not temporary
  - Not apologetic
  - Not inviting alternatives

This is a **verdict page without a product**.

## **Psychological State of the User**

A user who lands here is thinking:

1.  “Surely *something* should qualify”
2.  “Are they being lazy or extreme?”
3.  “Do they actually know what they’re doing?”

Your job is to:

  - Respect the instinct
  - Explain the structure
  - Hold the line calmly

## **Page Structure (Top → Bottom)**

The Empty page uses the **same skeleton** as a Declared Category Page.  
Only the *content* changes.

Consistency is critical.

## **1. Verdict Block (Above the Fold)**

**This is the most important part of the page.**

### **Required Elements**

1.  **Category Name**
      
      - e.g., *Refrigerator*, *Driver’s Car*
2.  **Status Badge**
      
      - **No Declaration**
3.  **One-Sentence Verdict**
      
      - Calm
      - Structural
      - Final (for now)

**Example (conceptual):**

**Verdict:** No currently available product meets the requirements of this category.

No apology.  
No “yet” unless you mean it.

## **2. Form Statement (Still Required)**

**Why this matters:****  
**You must show that you know *what the thing is*, even if nothing qualifies.

### **Rules**

  - Same rigor as declared categories
  - One sentence
  - Defines the essential function

This prevents the accusation:

“They don’t even know what they’re looking for.”

## **3. Why Nothing Qualifies (Structural Failure)**

**This replaces “Why This.”**

### **Purpose**

Explain **why the market fails the Form**, not why products are bad.

### **Required Characteristics**

  - Structural, not emotional
  - Market-wide, not brand-specific
  - Causes, not complaints

### **Common structural failure patterns**

  - Sealed systems
  - Proprietary electronics
  - Silent model drift
  - Regulatory pressure forcing disposability
  - Repair economics inverted

This section teaches the reader **how markets fail**, not how brands do.

## **4. Disqualifiers (Market-Level)**

**This replaces “Why Not Others.”**

Instead of listing alternatives, you list **patterns**.

### **Examples**

  - “Any model requiring proprietary diagnostics”
  - “Any design where a single control board failure disables the core function”
  - “Any product where repair exceeds 50% of replacement cost”

This reframes emptiness as *inevitable*, not ideological.

## **5. Failure Modes (Why Finality Is Impossible)**

**This is critical.**

You must show that:

  - Failure is expected
  - Failure is irreversible or irrational to fix
  - Therefore, finality cannot exist

This answers:

“But couldn’t you just pick the least bad one?”

No. Least bad ≠ Platonic.

## **6. Re-Declaration Triggers (Conditions for Reconsideration)**

**This is what separates seriousness from dogmatism.**

### **Required Content**

A clear list of **what would have to change** for a declaration to become possible.

Example (pattern):

  - A 15+ year parts guarantee
  - Modular electronics
  - Repair cost below a fixed ratio
  - Model stability over X years

This proves:

  - You are open to being wrong
  - But not to lowering the standard

## **7. What We Reviewed (Optional but Powerful)**

This is optional but recommended for high-stakes categories.

A short list of **archetypes examined**, not brands endorsed.

Example:

  - Commercial-style residential
  - Base mechanical models
  - Legacy pre-electronic designs

This shows effort without inviting comparison.

## **What the Empty Page Must Never Do**

  - Offer “best available”
  - Suggest compromises
  - Provide buying links
  - Ask for patience
  - Say “coming soon” unless truly planned

If you give consolation, you destroy the point.

## **Language & Tone Rules**

Empty pages must be:

  - Calm
  - Clinical
  - Non-performative
  - Non-defensive

They should feel like:

A safety engineer explaining why a bridge cannot be certified.

## **Success Criteria for Empty Pages**

An Empty Category Page succeeds if:

1.  Users stop arguing and start nodding
2.  Skeptics concede the logic even if they disagree
3.  Readers cite emptiness as a reason they trust the site
4.  The page would still make sense 5 years from now

## **Engineering & Content Notes**

  - Uses same template as declared pages
  - Status drives content visibility
  - Must be indexable (not hidden)
  - Must display “Last reviewed” date

## **Summary**

An Empty Category Page is not a gap.  
It is a **published conclusion** that the market has failed a real human need.

If you get these pages right, Platonic Ideal becomes **anti-fragile**.
